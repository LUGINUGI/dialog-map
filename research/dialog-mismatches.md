# Dialog: public conflict, private proximity

Updated: June 19, 2026

## Reading the evidence correctly

The leaked material is not one clean “membership list.” WIRED describes:

- a 113-person directory exposed in Dialog’s website code;
- a separate 2026 retreat registration list of 222 people;
- fields distinguishing “active member” from “guest”;
- attendance histories, including 87 first-time 2026 attendees.

Therefore, **directory appearance alone means Dialog retained a record for that person**. It does not prove current membership, attendance, friendship with Peter Thiel, agreement with other attendees, or secret coordination. Where Axios calls someone a “past participant,” that is stronger evidence of attendance.  

Core sources: [WIRED’s leak investigation](https://www.wired.com/story/leak-exposes-members-of-peter-thiels-secretive-dialog-society/), [WIRED on Dialog’s internal ranking system](https://www.wired.com/story/how-peter-thiels-private-dialog-club-secretly-ranks-its-members/), and [Axios on past participants](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/07/dialog-secret-network-thiel-hoffman).

## Strongest mismatches

### 1. Elon Musk and Reid Hoffman: public enemies, same private-network orbit

**Dialog evidence:** Both appear in reported Dialog material; Axios describes Reid Hoffman as appearing in Dialog sessions and Elon Musk as a past participant.  
**Public conflict:** Their PayPal-era association has deteriorated into fights over Trump, OpenAI, and each other’s Jeffrey Epstein contacts. In February 2026 they publicly traded accusations after records showed that both had communicated with Epstein after his conviction.  
**What it means:** Dialog proximity does not make the feud fake. It shows that elite forums can preserve access between people who publicly attack one another—and that the same people may remain socially or institutionally adjacent long after friendship ends.  
Sources: [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/epstein-island-emails-reignite-feud-between-elon-musk-and-reid-hoffman-a48703ff), [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/reid-hoffman-epstein-files-elon-musk-feud-2026-2), [Axios/Dialog](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/07/dialog-secret-network-thiel-hoffman).

### 2. Peter Thiel and Reid Hoffman: old friends and political nemeses

**Dialog evidence:** Thiel co-founded Dialog. Hoffman appears in its records and in Axios’s description of its sessions.  
**Public conflict:** The former Stanford and PayPal associates now finance opposing political projects. Hoffman says he speaks to Thiel less to avoid fighting over Trump; they reportedly had a tense public confrontation over Trump and Biden at Sun Valley in 2024.  
**What it means:** This is less a contradiction than a picture of elite continuity: political polarization damaged the friendship without fully dissolving their shared institutional world.  
Sources: [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/reid-hoffman-peter-thiel-politics-clash-dont-talk-trump-2023-3), [Puck](https://puck.news/peter-thiel-reid-hoffman-face-off-in-sun-valley/), [Hoover discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J2klGJRrjqw).

### 3. Elon Musk and Sam Harris: former friends turned personal antagonists

**Dialog evidence:** Both appear in the exposed Dialog directory; that alone does not prove they attended together.  
**Public conflict:** Harris says Musk defamed a former friend and amplifies misinformation. Musk called Harris “mentally ill” and an “utter idiot.” Their break reportedly grew from COVID disputes, politics, and Musk’s conduct on X.  
**What it means:** The interesting fact is not secret friendship but overlapping invitation networks. Dialog appears designed to retain ideologically and personally incompatible figures as potentially valuable participants.  
Sources: [Sam Harris, “The Trouble with Elon”](https://samharris.substack.com/p/the-trouble-with-elon), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/01/16/elon-musk-bet-covid-cases-sam-harris/), [WIRED/Dialog](https://www.wired.com/story/leak-exposes-members-of-peter-thiels-secretive-dialog-society/).

### 4. Ezra Klein and Sam Harris: a bitter intellectual dispute inside the same directory

**Dialog evidence:** Both appear in the exposed directory; joint attendance has not been established.  
**Public conflict:** Their 2018 debate over Charles Murray, race, IQ, identity, and journalistic responsibility became openly hostile. Harris accused Klein and Vox of smearing him; Klein argued Harris ignored the political and historical meaning of the claims he platformed.  
**What it means:** Dialog’s value proposition may be precisely this kind of unresolved conflict: opponents with large audiences, high status, and a demonstrated appetite for argument.  
Sources: [Vox debate transcript](https://www.vox.com/2018/4/9/17210248/sam-harris-ezra-klein-charles-murray-transcript-podcast), [Harris’s response](https://www.samharris.org/blog/ezra-klein-editor-chief).

### 5. Sophia Bush and Peter Thiel: attended a Thiel-founded forum while rejecting Thiel

**Dialog evidence:** Bush confirmed attending a Dialog conference as a guest.  
**Public conflict:** After the leak, she said Thiel was someone “you could not pay me to be in a room with.” She said he was not present, was never discussed during her experience, and she later learned he had not been involved for roughly 15 years.  
**What it means:** “Peter Thiel’s secret society” is an attention-grabbing but incomplete description of the current institution. At least some guests did not understand the founder connection and did not encounter Thiel.  
Sources: [Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/sophia-bush-defends-attending-peter-thiel-linked-secret-society-event-12003015), [WIRED/Dialog](https://www.wired.com/story/leak-exposes-members-of-peter-thiels-secretive-dialog-society/).

### 6. Joseph Gordon-Levitt and Peter Thiel: two conferences, no personal relationship

**Dialog evidence:** Gordon-Levitt confirmed attending two Dialog conferences.  
**Public conflict:** He said he has never met or spoken with Thiel and considers them political and ideological opposites. He described attendance as an attempt to engage people with different views about technology and AI.  
**What it means:** Confirmed attendance is still not evidence of affiliation with Thiel’s political project. It may instead reflect Dialog’s deliberate recruitment of countervoices.  
Source: [Entertainment Weekly quoting Gordon-Levitt’s statement](https://ew.com/josh-brolin-addresses-being-named-in-peter-thiel-secret-society-12002001).

### 7. Josh Brolin: internally evaluated despite apparent confusion about the organization

**Dialog evidence:** Records reportedly say Brolin was nominated by Uber CEO Ryan Graves and received an internal “C” score. His representative responded that Brolin wanted to know what he had gotten himself into.  
**Public/private mismatch:** Dialog’s database could contain detailed applications, nominations, and rankings while a listed person publicly expressed uncertainty about the organization.  
**What it means:** The internal system may know and judge more about prospective participants than those participants understand about Dialog’s governance and history.  
Sources: [Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/josh-brolin-addresses-being-named-in-peter-thiel-secret-society-12002001), [WIRED ranking investigation](https://www.wired.com/story/how-peter-thiels-private-dialog-club-secretly-ranks-its-members/).

### 8. Ted Cruz and Cory Booker: partisan opposites in the same forum

**Dialog evidence:** Axios identifies both senators as past Dialog participants; both also appear in the exposed directory.  
**Public conflict:** Cruz is a Trump-aligned conservative; Booker is a Democratic senator who has opposed Trump-world policies and Elon Musk’s DOGE project. They represent opposing coalitions on courts, taxation, immigration, abortion, and executive power.  
**What it means:** Cross-partisan attendance appears intentional, not accidental. The sharper question is whether off-the-record elite access creates a level of mutual availability that ordinary constituents and public-interest groups do not receive.  
Sources: [Axios/Dialog](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/07/dialog-secret-network-thiel-hoffman), [Booker on DOGE](https://www.booker.senate.gov/news/press/booker-colleagues-demand-answers-on-musks-unlawful-access-to-federal-payment-system).

### 9. Jared Kushner and Democratic officeholders: Trump-world access across party lines

**Dialog evidence:** Axios lists Kushner alongside Democratic figures including Cory Booker, Wes Moore, and Jared Polis as past participants or participants in Dialog’s orbit.  
**Public conflict:** Kushner was a central Trump adviser, while these Democrats have opposed major parts of Trump’s agenda.  
**What it means:** Dialog is not best understood as a simple right-wing membership organization. It is better described as a privately managed access network whose founders and some core figures lean right, while its utility depends on recruiting power from competing camps.  
Source: [Axios/Dialog](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/07/dialog-secret-network-thiel-hoffman).

### 10. Peter Thiel and Lawrence Summers: educational enemies sharing an elite forum

**Dialog evidence:** Thiel founded Dialog; Axios names Summers as a past participant.  
**Public conflict:** Summers called the Thiel Fellowship—which pays young people to leave or skip college—the most misdirected philanthropic initiative of its decade. Thiel’s project explicitly challenges institutions like Harvard that Summers led.  
**What it means:** Their common presence illustrates Dialog’s taste for high-status ideological opposition rather than a unified doctrine.  
Sources: [Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/flyby/article/2013/10/14/summers-thiel-misdirected/), [Axios/Dialog](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/07/dialog-secret-network-thiel-hoffman).

### 11. Peter Thiel and Eric Schmidt: stagnation critic versus establishment technocrat

**Dialog evidence:** Thiel founded Dialog; Axios identifies Schmidt as a past participant.  
**Public conflict:** In their well-known 2012 debate, Thiel attacked the narrowness of digital progress and mocked Schmidt’s position as “let them eat iPhones.” Schmidt defended Google-era innovation and later described himself as a Thiel fan despite their political differences.  
**What it means:** This is a real ideological clash without clear personal enmity—exactly the kind of disagreement Dialog claims to curate.  
Sources: [Fortune debate transcript](https://fortune.com/2012/07/17/transcript-schmidt-and-thiel-smackdown/), [Axios/Dialog](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/07/dialog-secret-network-thiel-hoffman).

### 12. Elon Musk and Eric Schmidt: opposing AI-risk narratives

**Dialog evidence:** Axios names both as past Dialog participants.  
**Public conflict:** Schmidt publicly said Musk was “exactly wrong” about catastrophic AI risk. Their disagreement reflects a deeper split between accelerationist industry policy and existential-risk warnings, although both support large-scale technological development.  
**What it means:** Shared elite access can coexist with material policy disagreement. It should not be simplified into friendship or coordination.  
Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/05/25/eric-schmidt-musk-exactly-wrong/), [Axios/Dialog](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/07/dialog-secret-network-thiel-hoffman).

### 13. Bret Stephens and Elon Musk: anti-Trump conservative versus MAGA-aligned platform owner

**Dialog evidence:** Both appear in the exposed directory; Axios independently identifies Musk as a past participant.  
**Public conflict:** Stephens has compared Musk’s style to Trump’s and criticized his social-media eruptions, hostility toward journalism, and reliance on grand promises. Musk moved into Trump’s political coalition; Stephens remains an anti-Trump conservative.  
**What it means:** “Right wing” is too crude a category for this network. Establishment conservatives, libertarians, MAGA populists, and neoconservatives can be direct opponents while remaining in the same invitation ecosystem.  
Sources: [Axios on Stephens’s Musk column](https://www.axios.com/2018/05/26/elon-musk-silicon-valleys-trump), [WIRED/Dialog](https://www.wired.com/story/leak-exposes-members-of-peter-thiels-secretive-dialog-society/).

### 14. Jim Himes and Joe Lonsdale: congressional oversight beside a major surveillance contractor

**Dialog evidence:** WIRED says both are listed; Himes confirmed attending one Dialog conference roughly a decade ago and denied further involvement.  
**Structural conflict:** Himes is the ranking Democrat on the House Intelligence Committee, which oversees agencies that contract with Palantir. Lonsdale co-founded Palantir, whose systems serve intelligence, defense, policing, and immigration agencies.  
**What it means:** This is more consequential than a celebrity feud. Private, off-the-record proximity between overseers and contractors creates a transparency concern even without evidence of improper discussion or influence.  
Sources: [WIRED/Dialog](https://www.wired.com/story/leak-exposes-members-of-peter-thiels-secretive-dialog-society/), [CT Insider](https://www.ctinsider.com/news/article/jim-himes-dialog-leaked-membership-list-thiel-22309787.php).

### 15. Regulators and data-industry executives: “avoid status signaling” inside a room built from status

**Dialog evidence:** WIRED reports that Dialog chairman Auren Hoffman—founder of SafeGraph and LiveRamp—appears alongside Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Senator Ted Cruz, whose committee oversees the FTC and data-privacy authority. The 2026 list also includes central-bank, Google DeepMind, defense, and national-security figures.  
**Public/private mismatch:** Dialog’s moderator guide tells participants to avoid status signaling, while its internal system grades people by wealth, fame, perceived value, relationships, and usefulness; those grades affect meetings, seating, price, and future invitations.  
**What it means:** This is the leak’s clearest institutional contradiction. Dialog publicly frames itself as candid cross-ideological inquiry, while privately operating a highly quantified hierarchy of elite value.  
Sources: [WIRED leak investigation](https://www.wired.com/story/leak-exposes-members-of-peter-thiels-secretive-dialog-society/), [WIRED ranking investigation](https://www.wired.com/story/how-peter-thiels-private-dialog-club-secretly-ranks-its-members/).

## Bottom line

The strongest supported interpretation is not that every public feud is theater or that Dialog is a unified conspiracy. The evidence instead shows a durable **elite access layer** beneath public conflict:

1. Ideological enemies can remain useful contacts.
2. Former friends can become public antagonists without leaving the same invitation ecosystem.
3. Guests may attend without knowing Dialog’s history or meeting its famous founder.
4. Officials and regulated industries can share confidential social space outside ordinary public-record systems.
5. Dialog’s internal conduct—ranking, relationship tracking, political profiling, matchmaking, and differential pricing—is more revealing than mere co-listing.

The defensible scandal is therefore **opacity plus concentrated access**, not proof that all participants share a hidden agenda.
